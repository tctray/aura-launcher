# AURA privacy policy

Last updated October 27, 2026

## The short version

AURA is a game launcher for Windows, developed by Taurrean Traylor. It has no ads and no tracking or analytics, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing.

Most of what AURA does stays on your PC. If you create an AURA account, the things listed below are saved online so they follow you to another PC and so friends can message you.

## What stays on your PC

- Clips, recordings and screenshots you make with AURA.
- Performance readings (frame rate, temperatures and the like).
- Your login, kept encrypted by Windows so you stay signed in.
- How you've set up the Messages page, including a background picture of your own.
- Which camera, microphone and speakers you chose, the sound devices you picked in Settings > Sound devices, and your Quick Switch favourites.
- Your music and video library in Clips > Media: the folders you added, your playlists, and copies AURA converts so a file can play. None of it is uploaded.
- Sign-ins and cookies for websites you open in AURA's Social browser (Instagram, X and others), kept the way any web browser keeps them.

## What is saved to your account

These are stored with Supabase, the database service AURA uses.

- Sign-up details: your email address, username and password. The password is stored scrambled; nobody, including the developer, can read it.
- Your profile: username, bio and profile picture. Anyone signed in to AURA can see these, which is how friends find you by username. A profile picture you choose from your PC is stored with its own web address, so anyone who has that address can see it.
- Your library: game titles, favorites, how often and how long you've played, and when you last played.
- Play sessions: the game, when it started and ended, and the game's file location on your PC. To notice a game you started outside AURA, AURA checks which programs are running on your PC and compares their names with your library. That check stays on your PC; only the sessions it finds are saved.
- Achievements, streaks, themes, settings, and your AURA background if it is a web address.
- Friends: the requests you send and receive, your friends, the people you block, and when you were last online. Only your friends see whether you're online.
- Messages: the text, pictures, GIFs and videos you send, link previews (a link's title, description and a small picture), the likes and dislikes you put on messages, a chat's shared background, and whether a message has been read.
- Group chats: the group's name, who is in it and who made it.
- Calls: who called whom, when, and whether the call was answered. This is kept for 30 days. The sound and picture of a call are never recorded or saved.
- Reports you send: the reason, anything you add, and a copy of the message you reported.

## Who can see your messages

A conversation between two people, and its files, can be opened only by those two people. A group chat and its files can be opened by everyone in the group, which can include people who aren't your friends. Files have no public links.

When you send a link, your PC visits that page once to make its preview, and the preview is saved with the message. The other person's AURA doesn't visit the page unless they open it.

Messages are protected on their way to and from AURA, but they are not end-to-end encrypted. The developer can access the database to keep AURA running and to look into reports. Don't send passwords, card numbers or anything else you'd need to keep secret.

## Calls

You can call your AURA friends with your voice and, if you choose, your camera, and you can share your screen. AURA uses your microphone, camera or screen only while you're in a call and have them switched on, or while you record a clip with the camera on.

When a call is accepted, the PCs connect to each other and the sound and picture travel between them, encrypted. They don't pass through AURA's database and they aren't recorded.

To connect, each PC is told the other's internet (IP) address. This happens only after a call is accepted, and only with the people in the call. In a call with three people, a friend can add one of their own friends, so you may connect to, and share your IP address with, someone who isn't your friend. If you'd rather not, leave the call.

On a few networks PCs can't connect directly. If AURA has a relay set up for that, the encrypted call passes through it instead. The relay can't listen to it or watch it.

## Sharing clips

When you press Share on a clip, AURA uploads it to Catbox (catbox.moe), a free file host, and gives you a link. Anyone with the link can watch the clip, and AURA can't delete it afterwards. If Catbox isn't taking uploads, the clip goes to Litterbox, run by the same people, and is deleted after 3 days.

A clip over 200 MB is made smaller on your PC before it's uploaded. Your clip itself isn't changed. Only share clips you're happy for anyone to see.

## Other services AURA talks to

AURA uses these to do its job. Each sees your internet address, as any website does, and has its own privacy policy.

- Supabase: accounts, the database, stored files and live updates.
- The AURA server (hosted by GoDaddy): fetches cover art, trailers, streams and Steam details for you, so the keys for those services aren't on your PC. It checks that you're signed in and doesn't keep a history of what you look up.
- IGDB and Twitch: game titles are sent to find cover art and live streams, and what you type in stream search. Streams play in Twitch's own player.
- YouTube: a game's title is sent to find its trailer. Trailers, YouTube links in messages, and YouTube videos and playlists in Clips > Media play in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player (youtube-nocookie.com).
- Catbox and Litterbox: clips you choose to share (see Sharing clips).
- Websites you send links to: your PC visits the page once to make a preview (see Who can see your messages).
- Steam: if you connect Steam, your Steam ID is used to show your profile, friends and playtime.
- Discord: if you connect Discord, you sign in with Discord, and AURA can show the game you're playing on your Discord profile.
- Google and Cloudflare: when a call connects, their free public servers tell your PC its own internet address, so the PCs can find each other. They learn nothing else about the call.
- KLIPY: when you use GIF search, what you type goes through the AURA server to KLIPY to find GIFs, along with a scrambled ID that stands for you (not your name, email or AURA account). A GIF picked from the search is not copied into AURA: the message holds its address at KLIPY, and both people's AURA loads it from KLIPY when the chat is opened.
- GitHub: AURA checks GitHub for new versions and downloads updates from it.
- Web3Forms: if you send feedback from Settings, your message, your AURA username, the version of AURA you're using, and an email address if you choose to give one, are sent through Web3Forms, which delivers them to the developer by email. Nothing is sent unless you press Send.

## Deleting things

- A message: point at one of your own messages and choose Delete. Its words and its file are removed for everyone in the chat.
- A friend: remove them from the profile card in a chat. Your past conversation stays, but neither of you can send more.
- Yourself from a group chat: choose Leave. The person who made the group can also remove people.
- Your account and everything in it: open Edit profile (under your name in the left panel) and choose Delete account. It's deleted straight away, with the messages you sent and your one-to-one chats. You can also email the address below from the email address on the account.
- A shared clip: AURA can't delete it from Catbox. Email the address below and say which link, and the developer will ask Catbox to remove it.
- Uninstalling AURA removes it from your PC. It doesn't delete your account.

## Blocking and reporting

Blocking someone removes them from your friends and stops them from messaging you, calling you or sending you friend requests. They aren't told.

Reporting someone sends the reason, and the message if you report one, to the developer. A reported message is kept for review even if its sender deletes it afterwards. Accounts used for harassment, spam or anything illegal may be removed.

## How long things are kept

What's saved to your account stays until you delete it or delete your account. Call records are kept for 30 days. Reports are kept for as long as it takes to deal with them.

## Children

AURA isn't meant for children under 13, and accounts shouldn't be created for them.

## Changes to this policy

If AURA starts storing something new or using another service, this page is updated and the date at the top changes.
